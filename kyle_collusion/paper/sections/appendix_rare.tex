To see why a myopic shared-table learner reacts to large noise shocks and to
deviations at $\xi=500$, we trained it once more under each price-state
binning (20 markets, $1.5\times10^7$ periods, otherwise as in
Table~\ref{tab:shared}), recorded which states greedy play visits, and
computed the greedy trading intensity in every state on a scale where 0 is the
cartel and 1 is Nash.

\paragraph{Noise-unit binning.} Greedy play visits only the two outermost
price-surprise bins (100\% of visits), because learners that trade below Nash
move the price hundreds of noise standard deviations from the Nash-expected
value. In those states the learned intensity is close to the cartel
(0.29--0.29). The thirteen interior bins are never visited, and their
greedy orders remain near their Nash-like initialisation (0.79--0.81). A
shock in the direction of $v$ moves the next state towards the interior; small
shocks do not reach it, large ones do, which produces the threshold.

\paragraph{Grid-range binning.} Greedy play now spreads over all fifteen
price bins (3.8--9.8\% of visits each), and the gradient is milder but has
the same sign: intensity is 0.30 in states visited more often than the
median, 0.35 in states visited less often, and 0.47 in (value, price) states
never visited in greedy play. Learned intensity also varies across price bins
(0.31--0.42) for reasons unrelated to rivals' behaviour, since with $\gamma=0$
each state's Q-values only estimate immediate profit.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} With pruning, how aggressively a Q-learner trades in
a state depends on how often, and how recently, that state has been updated:
heavily visited states are pruned most, rarely visited ones less, and
never-visited ones not at all. A shock or a rival's deviation moves the market
into a different price state, and the learner's trading changes with it. The
change looks like a reaction to the shock or the deviation, but it reflects
the uneven training of the states, not a strategy, which is why it appears in a
learner with $\gamma=0$. The general lesson does not depend on the binning:
whenever shocks or deviations move a Q-learner into less-trained states, its
reaction reflects initialisation and incomplete exploration, and only a
placebo can tell it apart from punishment.
