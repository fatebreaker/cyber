\paragraph{Shared Q-tables.} \citet{esquinas2026} find the price-trigger
signature sharpest when both traders read and update one shared Q-table.
Applying the placebo to that protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:additional}), a
\emph{myopic} shared-table learner reacts to noise shocks in proportion to
their size ($\SharedDouMyopicShockLarge\%$ of $\beta^N$ for a one-unit shock)
and its rivals trade harder after a forced deviation
($\SharedDouMyopicRival\pm\SharedDouMyopicRivalCI\%$), the patterns read as a
price trigger and as punishment, although with $\gamma=0$ it has no reason to
punish and deviating still pays. The forward-looking shared learner shows
neither reaction, at $1.5\times10^7$ or $2\times10^8$ periods.
